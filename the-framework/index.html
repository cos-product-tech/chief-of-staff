How each verdict was made

Three tests, applied to every term. Does the word carry a precise meaning with real consequences? A Staff engineer owns a domain; a Principal owns a workstream — use those words loosely and you've moved someone's job two levels. Is it ordinary English an engineer already knows? Career track, single point of failure, escaped defect — no tax. If it's an invented phrase, does it earn its keep? If plain English says the same thing, the invention is a tax on the reader, and it goes.

What the Cost column means. Changing a word costs different amounts depending on where it lives. FREE = ordinary page text; find-and-replace territory. MODERATE = also appears in the hiring pages' own text (interview loops, job descriptions), so the sweep is wider. EXPENSIVE = lives inside a role card's capability blocks. Card text is pasted verbatim into every evidence note and promotion packet in use, and block first sentences are quoted on the interview loop pages as "the bar" — so a card change means re-syncing loops and templates, and every manager's working copy goes stale until refreshed. Verified: all 29 loop pages quote only first sentences, never full blocks.

One name for the whole thing

Recommendation: the proper name becomes "Priority Commerce Engineering Expectations Framework" — "the Framework" for short. Today the pages drift between "the Engineering Expectations," "the framework," "the system," and "the map," and several page footers still carry an older working title. The pass standardizes all of it: the full name on first use and in titles, the Framework everywhere after, and the stray synonyms ("the system," "the map") retired. One legacy page from an earlier draft (titled "Engineering Career Framework," still using spine/rung vocabulary) gets folded in or archived — Eric's call, flagged in the pass.

Two standing rules so the dialect doesn't grow back

1 · Define on first use. Every structural term links its Glossary definition the first time it appears on a page. The Glossary already exists; the pages just don't lean on it consistently.
2 · A one-metaphor budget. One deliberate image per page, maximum. An invented phrase must either be in the Glossary or replaceable by plain English at no loss — if it's neither, it doesn't ship.
What the pass will not touch: role-card sentences. Card text is pasted verbatim into every evidence note and promotion packet, and each block's first sentence is quoted on its interview loop page — their stability is the framework's integrity. The only card-touching changes on this page are the ones explicitly put to Colm (start-left; the one two-lever sentence), and each would run with its full re-sync — card, loops, templates — verified against this count afterward.
Scope if approved as recommended: roughly 45 text edits across 12 core pages, 2 Evidence Note workbook cells, and the 2 published playbook twins; the rung/sanctioned cleanup on a handful of hiring pages and the legacy page; the brand standardization to "the Framework"; and — only with Colm's sign-off — the start-left re-sync (3 track pages, their loops, the templates). Zero card sentences change without explicit approval above. Every edit verified against the 77-page count after the pass.
