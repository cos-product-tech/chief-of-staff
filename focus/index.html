Office of the CTO · Chief of Staff · Focus to Date

Making a 300-person Product & Engineering
organization run as one system

The Chief of Staff role exists so the CTO's decisions turn into execution without the CTO carrying every thread. Since January, the focus has been on the people system, the money system, and the execution system: who we hire and keep, what we spend and with whom, and whether we deliver what we committed.

Internal · Confidential ⚡ Jan – Oct 2026 October 2026 · Eric Normant
Operating principle
Recommend, then act. Every decision that stops work, moves a customer date, adds spend, changes a vendor agreement, or affects a person comes to the CTO with a recommendation attached.

What the focus produced outcomes by area · January to October 2026

Engineering Competency Framework & Operating System

~40 items
  • ECF delivered for every engineering and QA role, mapped to the company ladder, with Distinguished Engineer and Principal tracks defined.
  • Skills assessments completed by managers alongside performance reviews, giving a baseline for every engineer.
  • Engineering maturity model and manager playbook set the bar for teams and leaders.

Hiring machinery

~70 items
  • One information architecture for job descriptions, interview loops, and scorecards, so every open role starts from a standard.
  • Headcount reconciled across HR, Finance, and P&E budgets ahead of the FY27 cycle; every open role tied to a ticket and a budget line.
  • Geography strategy executed: Waterloo tech hub cost-benefit, salary bands, and search partner; IDC and LATAM pipelines reviewed role by role.

Retention & performance

~40 items
  • Retention strategy built from post-review sentiment, with compensation recommendations funded through Finance and HR for the engineers we could least afford to lose.
  • Annual review cycle run end to end: calibration across all of Engineering, review discussions, merit validation, follow-ups.
  • Performance management handled with care, including transitions, backfills, and continuity plans for single points of failure.

Organizational design

~15 items
  • Platform reorganization designed and communicated: leader-by-leader alignment, the CTO's announcement, and 1:1s with every affected person.
  • Reporting lines clarified for QA and SDET, hiring-manager ownership, and a proposed Central Services structure.
  • Workstream definition kept current so teams, budgets, and metrics all describe the organization the same way.

Vendors & the money system

~40 items
  • Agreements closed: two master services agreements and three statements of work, including the ERP platform build, through P&E, Finance, and Legal review.
  • Milestone payment schedules established with Finance for vendor-built products, so spend tracks delivery.
  • Budget moved to need: roles re-leveled and re-sited, incremental hires mapped to budget lines, capitalization questions answered.

Execution & engineering health

~45 items
  • Engineering metrics live: DORA setup, roster and cost accuracy, manager playbook and training, Q2 developer experience survey.
  • Weekly executive reporting for P&T, the Commitment Register for every P0, and the Quality & Release transformation charter.
  • Execution Command Board built: an AI-assisted live view of status, dependencies, accountability, and revenue impact for up to 100 initiatives.
