Office of the CTO · Chief of Staff · Focus to Date

Making a 300-person Product & Engineering
organization run as one system

The Chief of Staff role exists so the CTO's decisions turn into execution without the CTO carrying every thread. Since January, the focus has been on the people system, the money system, and the execution system: who we hire and keep, what we spend and with whom, and whether we deliver what we committed.

Internal · Confidential ⚡ Jan – Oct 2026 October 2026 · Eric Normant
Operating principle
Recommend, then act. Every decision that stops work, moves a customer date, adds spend, changes a vendor agreement, or affects a person comes to the CTO with a recommendation attached.

What the focus produced outcomes by area · January to October 2026

Engineering Competency Framework & Operating System

~40 items
  • One standard for every engineering and QA role, mapped to the company ladder with Distinguished Engineer and Principal tracks, so hiring, calibration, and promotion decisions are judged the same way everywhere.
  • A skills baseline for every engineer, completed by managers alongside performance reviews, so development plans and staffing decisions start from evidence.
  • An eight-dimension team maturity model and manager playbook that set the bar for teams and leaders and give managers a coaching agenda, not just a review form.

Hiring machinery

~70 items
  • Every open role starts from a standard: one information architecture for job descriptions, interview loops, and scorecards, so interviewers assess the same things and candidates get a consistent bar.
  • One hiring plan that ties across HR, Finance, and P&E: 43 open roles reconciled ahead of the FY27 cycle, each tied to a ticket and a budget line.
  • Geography strategy executed: Waterloo hub business case, salary bands, and search partner; IDC and LATAM pipelines reviewed role by role.

Retention & performance

~40 items
  • Retention strategy built from evidence: post-review sentiment identified the engineers we could least afford to lose, and compensation recommendations were funded through Finance and HR.
  • Annual review cycle run end to end: calibration across all of Engineering, review discussions, merit validation, and follow-ups.
  • Performance management handled with care, including transitions, backfills, and continuity plans that removed single points of failure on critical systems.

Organizational design

~15 items
  • Four platform domains established (Surfaces & Applications, Commerce API, Commerce Core, Bedrock) so each team owns something meaningful and dependencies are visible; aligned leader by leader, communicated by the CTO, followed by 1:1s with every affected person.
  • Reporting lines clarified for QA and SDET, hiring-manager ownership, and a proposed Central Services structure.
  • One workstream definition kept current so teams, budgets, and metrics all describe the organization the same way.

Vendors & the money system

~40 items
  • Agreements closed: two master services agreements and three statements of work, including the ERP platform build, through P&E, Finance, and Legal review.
  • Spend tracks delivery: milestone payment schedules established with Finance for every vendor-built product.
  • Budget moved to need: roles re-leveled and re-sited, incremental hires mapped to budget lines, capitalization questions answered.

Execution & engineering health

~45 items
  • Engineering metrics live for every team: DORA setup, roster and cost accuracy, manager playbook and training, Q2 developer experience survey, and one agreed definition of sprint predictability.
  • Leadership sees commitments without asking: weekly executive reporting for P&T, the Commitment Register for every P0, and the Quality & Release transformation charter.
  • Execution Command Board MVP built: an AI-assisted live view of status, dependencies, accountability, and revenue impact for up to 100 initiatives, with the reasoning behind each status visible and a human in the loop.
