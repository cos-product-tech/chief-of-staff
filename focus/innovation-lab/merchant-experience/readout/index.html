What you told us · In person

The top five things we heard in conversation.

From one-on-ones and small-group sessions with product, design, engineering, and quality.

  1. 01
    What’s working

    You want to keep getting better.

    You asked for more: time learning AI from each other, earlier involvement in the work, and contact with merchants.

  2. 02

    The date often arrives before the why.

    Requests land with a deadline attached and little context on the problem, the value, or why now.

  3. 03

    Design, engineering, and quality want in at the start.

    The best time to shape the work is at kickoff, before tickets exist. Today most of us join after that.

  4. 04

    There’s nowhere to test end to end before a shared environment.

    Without a full local stack, testing happens in environments other work is also moving through.

  5. 05

    Regression gets redone, and production is a month away.

    Changes land in UAT mid-regression, and a finished change takes about a month to reach a merchant.

What we saw · In action

The top five things we learned by sitting in.

From sitting in on sprint planning, grooming, standups, and retros over the past four weeks.

  1. 01
    What’s working

    Your own fixes are working.

    Grooming improved from one retro to the next, retro actions got done, and people speak up when something is off.

  2. 02

    Work reaches the sprint before it’s ready.

    Our most frequent observation. Stories get clarified and estimated in planning, so planning does the grooming.

  3. 03

    Capacity isn’t in the plan yet.

    Sprints are planned without a capacity number, carry-over goes in last, and new work arrives after we commit.

  4. 04

    There’s no discovery ritual yet.

    Product, design, and engineering have no regular time together with merchants, so questions surface mid-sprint.

  5. 05

    A lot rests on a few people.

    Grooming, estimates, and know-how lean on a handful of teammates. That’s a gap in the system, not in the people.

Three ways of listening, one picture: the craft is strong, and the system around it slows us down.

So we change the system, together. Some of it we can start now. Some of it we’ll need partners for.
What we’ll change now

Five changes we can start ourselves.

These are within our own control. We’d like to be working this way by Sprint 24.

01

Continuous discovery

Our lead product manager, product designer, principal software engineer, and quality lead work as one discovery team, every week.

  • Find 1 to 3 reference merchants we can learn from directly.
  • Configure FullStory so it gives us insight we can use.
  • Sprint planning prioritizes only work that meets the definition of ready, and every team member knows how we’ll tell if it succeeded.
  • Track key results and KPIs, and bring the team a regular read on how our products and features are performing.

02

Sprint planning

  • Capacity is respected. Nobody is booked over 100%.
  • Carry-over first.
  • Nothing is added after we commit unless something equal comes out.
  • Business partners stay informed. Every sprint they hear what’s planned, then what was delivered, through a bi-weekly sprint demo and an announcement.

03

An agent for every stage. A person for every gate.

Spec, design, architecture, build, review, and QA each get an agent. People are the gates: they approve the work or send it back.

04

Let the pipeline be the reviewer of first resort.

For MXM:

  • A standard merge checklist, enforced in CI.
  • Failing tests block the build.
  • Feature flags, so releases stop being all or nothing.

What we must aspire to

Three changes we’ll need partners for.

They reach beyond our team, and they set the ceiling on everything above.

01

No deployment into UAT during an active regression without notice.

A rule across workstreams, not a MerCap habit. It protects work already done, and it costs other teams one Slack message.

Tiger Team
Eric, Tony

02

A local stack for MXM

Without it, agents can’t run end to end and engineers deploy without seeing the whole picture, which adds regression issues and regression time.

03

Retire the DIP and shorten the path to production.

Today it takes about a month to get value to our merchants, with a hand-edited release plan and a 30-person call. A PR merged in hours means little if a merchant waits four weeks to feel it.
