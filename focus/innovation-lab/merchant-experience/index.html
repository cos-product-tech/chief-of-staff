Why, what, and the gate

Why now

Our business partners don't trust our commitments and see us as slow. AI tooling alone won't change that; a team that commits against unknown capacity and reports late will miss the same way, faster. So this runs two tracks together: the Lab's proven AI workflows, and the planning and commitment habits that create the trust gap.

What done looks like

Merchant Experience shows measurable improvement against its baseline on all six dimensions, read from telemetry only. Every engineer works in the AI-DLC workflow. A documented rollout playbook exists that Payables reads cold and runs without the Lab in the room, starting March 15.

The gate: outcomes before velocity

Velocity without value is the failure mode. Before any workflow changes, Alex and Erin, the Product Lead, agree two or three business outcomes the team must move in this window. Every sprint reports both columns. If speed rises and outcomes don't, we stop and ask why. Trust is rebuilt one kept commitment at a time, so we also track commitments shipped on the date promised.

Phases each with an exit test

Phase 0 · Oct 13–23

Prepare

  • Meetup: Bart, Amrita, Alex, Eric. Lab brings the two or three practices it would bet on; Alex brings where the team loses time.
  • Eric partners with Andrey from Jellyfish to build the baseline dashboard on all six dimensions. Review it with Bart, Amrita, and Alex first, then with Purush, Quan, and Andrew. The org-wide baseline replaces it on Nov 16.
  • Agree the two or three business outcomes with Alex and Erin.
  • Brief the team through Purush, Quan, Andrew, and Alex.
ExitBaseline dashboard live · outcomes named · team briefed
Phase 1 · Oct 26 – Nov 20

Embed and observe one full cycle

  • Eric and Amrita in every ritual alongside Alex for four weeks: Sprint 26 from planning through development, regression and QA, and its Nov 20 release.
  • Watch: how work enters and how much is validated first · commitments and the capacity behind them · mid-sprint additions · where time goes between started and done · what regression catches and how long it takes · how defects flow back · where decisions live · who talks to whom · what the team already uses AI for.
ExitObserved losses ranked by cost, with evidence from a whole cycle
Phase 2 · Nov 23 – Dec 4

Diagnose and design

  • Two weeks, with Thanksgiving inside: a design week that tolerates thin attendance rather than a workflow launch that doesn't.
  • Amrita pairs with each software engineer on the team, one by one, so every engineer has tried the Lab's workflow on their own code before Dec 7.
  • Map Lab practices onto the observed losses; pick three or four. Start where engineers feel the pain and review cost is low: tests, regression authoring, docs, boilerplate, PR descriptions. Agentic generation on core logic comes later.
  • At least one practice aimed at the regression window; it's half of every cycle.
  • Fix the commitment habit: capacity known before planning, no mid-sprint additions without a trade, weekly one-line note to business partners.
  • Agree measurement rules with Purush, Quan, Andrew.
ExitPractices chosen · rules agreed · go / no-go for Dec 7
Phase 3 · Dec 7 – Jan 15

Run the new workflow

  • Start at the Sprint 29 boundary with full participation. Amrita embedded daily; Lab engineers pairing through Sprints 29 and 30, since the holiday splits them.
  • Alex plus one or two engineers who take to it become in-team champions; the Lab pairs with them, they pair with the rest.
  • Expect a dip in Sprint 29. Continue unless it persists through Sprint 30.
  • Sprint 29's regression runs through the holiday and its release slips to the week of Jan 4; it is not counted in the result. Sprint 30 ships Jan 29: the first counted release.
  • Measure every sprint against baseline and outcomes; publish to the team and its leaders, good or bad.
Exit · Dec 31 checkpointTeam fully on the workflow · Sprint 29 built the new way and in regression · trend visible · outcomes on track
Phase 4 · Jan 18 – Mar 12

Prove and document

  • Full workflow, Lab out of the room, Alex and the champions teaching.
  • Four clean cycles ship: Sprints 30, 31, 32, and 33 on Jan 29, Feb 12, Feb 26, and Mar 12.
  • Playbook drafted from the first two counted cycles and finished by Feb 26: prerequisites, setup, the workflow step by step, guardrails, metrics, what went wrong and how it was fixed.
  • Tested cold: Payables reads it and says what's missing before its Mar 15 start.

What the evidence says and what it changed in the plan

AI amplifies what's already there

Throughput rises, but instability rises too unless foundations are solid: small batches, version control discipline, a quality platform, a clear AI stance, tools connected to internal code and docs.

DORA 2025 · ~5,000 respondents · why small batches and commitment discipline are part of the design
Expect a dip, and don't trust self-report

Experienced developers took 19% longer with AI tools on code they knew well, while believing they were 20% faster. Time went to checking and correcting.

METR 2025 RCT · why Sprint 29 may be slower and only telemetry counts
The realistic prize is moderate

Across 2M+ PRs, AI-assisted work ran about 16% faster; a joint study with OpenAI projected up to 24% at full adoption. Bug tickets rose 27% at the highest adoption in one study.

Jellyfish 2025 · why targets are 15–20% and quality is a guard
Access is not adoption

About 84% of developers have a tool; about 45% use one weekly. Rollouts stall on change management: fast first-week exploration, then drop-off. The ones that stick start on work engineers dislike and build peer champions.

Practitioner studies 2025–26 · why champions, task choice, and active-use telemetry
