Office of the CTO · Modernizing the tech delivery engine · Starting with Merchant Experience

Make Merchant Experience fast and predictable
on the commitments it makes to the business

This is broader than AI-native development. It is about modernizing the system the team works inside: operating rhythms, Jellyfish data turned into action, process and organizational drag, and whatever is slowing the team down. Across the industry that is usually waiting (on reviews, approvals, QA, environments), missing context (unclear requirements, hard-to-find information), interruptions (meetings, tool switching, mid-sprint changes), technical debt and complexity, and cross-team dependencies. We will find out which of these it is here. AI-DLC is one lever among several.

Internal · Confidential ⚡ P0 · Interviews by Oct 23 October 2026 · Eric Normant
The problem we are solving, together
The business needs Merchant Experience to deliver faster and more predictably on the commitments it makes. The team works hard inside a delivery system that makes that harder than it should be. We are going to fix the system, with the team, not around it.
What we will find out first
Where the time actually goes, and whether the team has the context it needs: the business, the customer, the problem being solved, and the KPI that would show an outcome was achieved. Where that context is missing, we supply it, and from then on no commitment is made without it.
How we will know
PrimaryA commitment is kept when it ships on the date promised.
SecondaryEvery new commitment states a hypothesis: we believe shipping this for this customer will move this KPI from its value today to a target, because of this reason. Without the hypothesis it is a feature; with it, it is a bet the team can learn from.
TertiaryThe team owns its KPIs: they sit on the team's own dashboard, the team looks at them every sprint, and for every shipped commitment the team can say whether the KPI moved and what it will do next.

Scope stay focused on the problem

In scope

  • Operating rhythms: how work enters, how commitments are made and against what capacity, how status flows to the business.
  • Jellyfish data used to find and act on specific drag.
  • The drag the industry sees most and we will look for first: waiting on reviews, approvals, QA, and environments; unclear requirements and hard-to-find information; interruptions from meetings, tool switching, and mid-sprint changes; technical debt and complexity; and handoffs and silos between engineering, QA, product, design, and the business.
  • Whether the team has the context behind each commitment, the business, the customer, the problem, and the KPI, and making sure every commitment carries that context from the day it is made.
  • How the team responds to business asks: capacity checks, trades, and how the business hears about changes. We change the team's side of that exchange, not the business's.
  • AI-DLC, paired in by the Innovation Lab, where it removes observed friction.

Out of scope, deliberately

  • Turning Merchant Experience into a product-led team. At Priority Commerce our business partners drive product; changing that is a longer transformation than this problem needs, and it would take too long.
  • Reorganizing the team or changing its leaders as a lever in this window. If structure turns out to be the constraint, that goes to the CTO as a recommendation.
  • Redesigning the sprint and regression cadence. Shortening regression through automation and AI is in scope; replacing the cadence is not.
  • Anything that needs business partners to change how they work. Those ideas go to a parking lot for the CTO, not into this plan.

Phase 2

Distill, embed, observe, decide

Oct 26 – Nov 20
  • Distill the interviews into a ranked list of what slows the team down, each item traced to the Jellyfish measure that would show it, sorted into waiting, missing context, interruptions, technical debt and complexity, and cross-team dependencies, and where context about the business, customer, problem, or KPIs is missing. Keep the developers' view and the managers' view separate; where they differ, that gap is itself a finding.
  • Eric, Amrita, Bart, and Steve in every ritual alongside Alex for one full cycle: Sprint 22 from planning through development, regression and QA, and its Sunday, Nov 22 release.
  • Trace two or three real items end to end in Jira and Jellyfish, from pickup through development, review, QA, regression, and release, and record how long each one waited at every step and why. Wait time, not work time, is usually most of the lead time. Compare what people said in interviews with what the Jellyfish benchmark dashboard and the observation show.
  • Watch commitments being made: against what capacity, with what information, and how the business hears about changes.
  • During the regression weeks, Nov 9–20: decide the ideas we will implement against the problem: no more than three or four, each aimed at a named constraint, at least one aimed at the regression window.
  • Fix the commitment habit as one of them: capacity known before planning, no mid-sprint additions without a trade, every commitment written as a hypothesis (customer, KPI, value today, target, reason) with a leading indicator the team can see move within weeks when the KPI itself is slow, the team's KPIs on the team's own dashboard and reviewed every sprint, and a weekly one-line note to business partners on what was committed, what shipped, and what moved.
ExitDrag ranked by cost with evidence from interviews, data, and observation · the two or three biggest constraints named · changes chosen and written down by Nov 20

Phase 4

Run the changes, then re-baseline

Dec 7 – Feb 16

Risks and decision rules named now so no one argues later

Scope creep into a product transformation. Everything we find will tempt us to fix how product is driven.If a change needs business partners to work differently, it goes to the CTO parking lot. This plan changes what the team controls, including the context every commitment carries.
Output without outcome. A date kept on a feature that moved nothing isn't success yet.Secondary objective; the date comes first. No commitment is made without a named KPI and its value today; the re-baseline reports which ones moved alongside which ones shipped, and a miss on the KPI goes back to the business as a question about the next bet.
KPIs too slow to read in this window. Revenue and adoption move over quarters; four sprints is weeks.Each commitment names a leading indicator the team can see move within weeks, alongside the KPI. The re-baseline checks the leading indicators; the KPIs are checked 90 days after each release and reported once the data is in.
Leaders and developers seeing different problems. Industry surveys consistently find developers believe their leaders don't see their real friction.Synthesize the individual contributors' interviews separately from the managers'. Where the two disagree, observation in Phase 2 settles it, and the gap is reported as a finding.
Interviews that stay polite. Twelve people, several of them each other's managers.One on one, thirty minutes, synthesized without attribution. Observation in Phase 2 checks what was said against what happens.
Fixing symptoms instead of the constraint. The first visible problem is rarely the biggest one.The end-to-end traces and the Jellyfish benchmark dashboard decide, not the most vivid interview. No more than three or four changes, each tied to a named constraint.
Data nobody trusts. Q3 definitions were still moving; a disputed baseline sinks the re-baseline.Dashboard and definitions agreed before Oct 26 with everyone we interview: Erin, Sharada, Kyle, Andrew, Mamatha, Alex, Ryan, Leonel, Ramya, Michael, Earnest, and Anzhelika. If Q3 is unusable, Sprints 22 and 23 are the baseline.
The dip. New ways of working are slower before they're faster.Continue unless the dip persists through Sprint 26; then revisit the changes, not the plan.
Holiday capacity. Sprint 26 and the regression windows around it run through the holidays, so two of the four sprints carry thinner data.The re-baseline reads the four sprints as one block against the baseline block; no single sprint decides it.
