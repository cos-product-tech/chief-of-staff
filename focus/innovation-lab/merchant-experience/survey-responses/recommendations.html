Rebuild the factory, not just the motor.

The code got faster. The factory didn't. Thirteen surveys, many conversations and rituals, and a sprint predictability of 18.8% say the same thing: work arrives unclear, plans change mid-sprint, and the path to production is long. These are the changes we bring to the team, ordered by what moves first, and redesigned around agents instead of bolted onto the old process.

The lesson

When factories got electric motors, productivity didn't move for thirty years.

They bolted the new motor onto the old line shafts and belts and kept the layout the same. The gains came only with unit drive: a motor on every machine, which made the old structure unnecessary and let the factory be redesigned around the work.

Most teams run AI the first way. A coding assistant sits inside an unchanged process: the same ceremonies, the same ticket queues, the same handoffs from product to engineering to QA. Drafts get faster. Nothing structural changes, because the constraint was never typing speed. It was the shape of the process.

MerCap shows both halves of this. AI has already made writing the code fast. What's slow is the intake, the plan, the handoffs, and a path to production that takes a month.

The question isn't whether to use agents. It's whether we'll redesign the work around them, or keep running the old belts.

What it means for MerCap

Three rules for everything on this page.

I

Wire the building first.

Unit drive needs power at every machine. Here that's an environment to test in, a pipeline that enforces its checks, and a path to production shorter than a month. Without them, agents have nowhere to run.

The wiring →
II

Move people to the gates.

Agents draft the spec, the code, the tests, the review brief. People approve the spec, the architecture, and the merge, against clear pass and send-back criteria. Gates nobody fails get automated.

01 and 04 →
III

Measure the building, not the motor.

Not effort, points, or licenses. Time from request to customer, rework at each gate, cost per shipped change, and how fast a fix for a recurring failure lands. Then improve it every week.

Why this

Every seat says work arrives unclear, and "work arrives unready" is the largest theme we observed. The fix is not another grooming meeting. It is a gate with clear pass and rework criteria: problem, outcome and KPI, acceptance criteria, the technical questions answered, design signed off end to end, test data named, other teams named. An agent drafts that from the request; a person spends their attention approving it, not writing it.
