What stands out five findings from one response · the seat that should be in discovery, describing its absence

01 · Three picks, all upstream

Every delay he named happens before design starts.

The problem and outcome are unclear; requirements, acceptance criteria, specs, or readiness are incomplete when work is planned; dates are committed before the solution, design scope, capacity, feasibility, or dependencies are understood. Nothing about handoffs, prototyping, QA, or incidents. From the design seat, the work is late before it arrives.

Q1: 3 of 13 options · the same three themes as the PMs and QA
02 · Zero of ten

None of the discovery practices are consistently true.

Ten statements describing discovery done well: understanding the problem first, a hypothesis, a baseline and target, early validation with merchants, feasibility and viability weighed before the solution is fixed, design involved early, a smaller first release, clear specs to engineering, an outcome review. Kyle checked none. So did both product managers.

Q2: 0/10 · PM: "none of these" 2/2 · the three discovery seats agree there is no discovery
03 · The weakest option

When design joins depends on who is asking.

Offered six descriptions from "design is involved during discovery, before the solution and date are committed" down to "involvement varies significantly depending on the initiative or stakeholders," Kyle chose the last. Read with the calendar: he sees the ceremonies on the shared MerCap calendar but is not a named invitee, and his design standup overlaps at 12:30 ET. He was out Monday and reached Tuesday's pre-grooming only at the end. He confirmed in person that Product, Design, and Engineering leads do no discovery together.

Q3: option 6 of 6 · confirmed with Kyle, Oct 27
04 · AI

AI is saving time on production, and absent from discovery.

Saves time on wireframing and prototyping, specs and handoffs, and documentation. Exploring on research, problem framing, the design system, and feasibility conversations. Not using on usability testing and post-launch analysis, the two activities that need users and outcomes the team doesn't have access to.

Q4: 3 saves time · 4 exploring · 2 not using · 0 unclear benefit
05 · Passkey, again

Design was working while the boundaries were still being decided.

Setup, fallback options, and management permissions were still open questions "while design was underway." The avoidable delay was the boundary between Keycloak's login and setup and the management experience owned by our products. His fix: agree one end-to-end flow with ownership and decision points at scoping. Planning saw the SMS gap; the retro heard Michael describe the back-and-forth; Kyle names the cause.

Read: the one option that describes no process at all. The five above it are a ladder from discovery to rework; "varies" means the team is on a different rung each time, decided by whoever brings design in. He is not a named invitee on the ceremonies, so the default rung is the bottom one.
