What stands out four findings from one response · a single seat, read as that person's view of the system

01 · One pick

The only delay named is cross-team dependencies.

Offered twelve sources of delay and up to three picks, Josette picked one: cross-team dependencies, external systems, or shared resources. Nothing about unclear problems, late alignment, capacity, readiness, or testing. From the program seat, the plan is sound and the dependencies are what break it.

Q1: 1 of 12 options · matches QA's loudest verbatim theme
02 · Eight of nine

From the program seat, almost every planning practice holds.

Hypothesis on every commitment, early cross-functional alignment, an integrated plan to the point of usable value, capacity validated before dates, documented risks and owners, explicit trade-offs when work changes, forecasts adjusted against actuals. Eight of nine statements checked. QA checked two or three.

Q2: 8/9 · compare the QA tab's 0/3 on hypothesis and on clear requirements
03 · The one unchecked

Nobody, including the TPM, reviews the KPI after release.

The single statement Josette left unchecked is the same one all three QA engineers left unchecked: after release, the team reviews the relevant KPI and decides what to do next. The plan runs to the release date and stops.

Q2: 0/1 · QA: 0/3 · the one unanimous gap across both surveys
04 · AI

AI is already working in the program seat, mostly on the paperwork.

Faster and better for status, meeting notes, decisions, actions, and documentation; better quality for risk tracking, Jira and Jellyfish analysis, and KPI monitoring; saves time on release readiness. Still exploring on forecasting, dependencies and critical path, and scope trade-offs, which are the judgment calls at the center of the role.

Q4: 6 of 9 activities positive · 3 exploring · 0 not using
