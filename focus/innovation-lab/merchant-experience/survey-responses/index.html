What ten people told us five findings across five seats · twelve people · read as the brief for Sprint 22

01 · Eleven of eleven

Work arrives unclear.

Everyone who receives or writes the work says so: an unclear problem (QA, director), incomplete requirements (engineers, PM), or no definition of ready. The Technical Program Manager, who plans the work, did not pick it.

QA 3/3 · engineers 4/5 · EM 1/1 · PM 2/2 · TPM 0/1
02 · Every seat

Cross-team dependencies cost real time, and three people proposed the same fix.

PCE (Commerce API) and Passkey are the named cases: a library that fit neither stack, a widget not yet deployed, three teams on three design flows. Quan, Avni, and Alex each propose aligning the teams before the work is assigned. Erin proposes a person to do it. Preston adds the DMS report: miscommunication between PRTH and DMS, and work thrown onto a plate that was already full.

named by all five seats · the one theme the survey options under-asked
03 · Twelve of twelve

Nobody reviews the outcome after release.

No seat says the KPI is reviewed after shipping. Both product managers, who own the hypothesis and the KPI, checked "none of these are consistently true"; Erin was the first to call the missing review a delay: decisions wait on evidence that is never collected.

0/12 · the value half of speed to value is unmeasured
04 · The craft is fine

Engineers own their quality, and AI has already moved their bottleneck.

Four of five engineers and two of three QA say engineers test their own work. Engineers are "faster and better" with AI on writing and testing code; Ramya is positive on all ten activities. In one engineer's words: AI made writing the code fast; what's slow now is working out how the pieces fit together, and that happens after the work has started.

quality ownership 6/8 · AI positive on 4 of 10 engineering activities, 5/5
05 · The gap between seats

The TPM describes an integrated plan with a hypothesis on every commitment, and says work arrives clear. Ten of the eleven people downstream say the opposite on both.

Both can be true if the plan does not travel to the ticket. Ramya is the one downstream voice who sees a hypothesis and an integrated planning model; her teammates don't, and her follow-up should ask which initiative she meant. Optimism about how a date gets made rises with every step away from the ticket.

Early candidates for the Nov 20 list what the surveys point to · to be tested against Sprint 22 and the Jellyfish baseline, not decided

A kickoff rule for cross-team work. No initiative that touches another team starts until the involved teams have agreed the requirement and each team's responsibility. The fix three people proposed; the Passkey and PCE cases are the test.
A definition of ready that carries the hypothesis. Problem, expected outcome, KPI with baseline and target, acceptance criteria, and the technical questions answered, before a story enters sprint planning. Probes 01 and 14 show how far today's definition is from that.
Design time before coding. A short technical design step for anything with cross-team or architectural dependencies, so discovery stops happening mid-ticket. The engineers' ask; the AI-native PDLC's natural home.
Reliable test data in lower environments. Earnest lost a week and a half to it; Preston says it is "still a problem" a year after the DMS report. Probe 04 times one story end to end; if it repeats, this is the most concrete fix on the list and the first place to aim AI help.
Feature flags in the Checkout API. Statsig is POC'd; two customer-impacting I1s trace to its absence. Small, concrete, and already scoped by Alex.
An outcome review after every release. The team reads the KPI it set out to move and decides what to do next. Twelve of twelve say it doesn't happen; it is the plan's future scope, and the PM seat is where it starts.
